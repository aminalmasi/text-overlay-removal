Erasing the text sellers type over their photos

Marketplace sellers burn prices, phone numbers and captions into their listing photos. This pipeline finds what was added, repaints what it covers, and leaves the photograph and its real-world writing alone.

The problem

The overlay is the image now

Listing photos arrive with the price, a phone number, the shop's name or an app watermark typed straight onto the pixels. There is no layered original to go back to and the goods were often sold long ago, so the only way to a clean photo is to reconstruct what the text covers. Roughly one listing photo in twenty carries text added on top of a real photograph.

Not all text is an overlay

Goods have writing on them: labels, packaging, shop signs, licence plates, screens. A text detector cannot tell the two apart. CRAFT fires on 85 of 86 photos that have no overlay at all, so the decision has to come from a model that understands the picture.

The overlay hides its own extent

Text usually sits on a chip, a band or a translucent panel. A mask drawn around the letters stops inside that carrier and leaves a rim; one drawn too wide repaints the product.

Repainting invites invention

An inpainting model fills a hole with what it expects to see there. Given a banner-shaped hole on a marketplace photo, it expects a banner, and paints new letters into it.

The system

Two cheap questions, then the GPU

Every photo gets one call to Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, the gate. Its whole answer is about 12 tokens: is there text worth storing, and was text added on top of a real photograph? Only photos with an overlay go on to the worker, which copies the text and boxes every added region and line, and then to the GPU eraser. Photos whose text is the content (screenshots, infographics, posters) go to the marketplace's existing text prompt and are never erased.

What the model is asked

Two prompts, and what they return

The prompt texts stay private. What matters for the pipeline is what each prompt asks and the fields it returns, because the eraser is built on those fields.

Gate: two questions about every photo

eligible (true or false): does the picture carry digital text? That means overlays such as prices, phone numbers and links, infographics, and screenshots of specification pages or messages. Writing on physical things never counts: printing, logos, stickers, licence plates, documents beside the product. A brand printed on a box is out; the same brand typed on as an overlay is in. The field keeps the meaning the marketplace already stores, so the gate can take over that call.

removal (0 or 1): was something added on top of a real photograph, so that removing it leaves a hole to reconstruct? The prompt lists what is 0 even though it carries text: text on its own band beside the photos, posters and adverts built around their words, cut-out product shots, screenshots, and app buttons and counters. Two rules settle the hard cases: "the amount of writing is not evidence" and "when unsure, 0".

{"eligible": true, "removal": 1}   // about 12 tokens

Worker: the text, and a box for every added mark

Runs only when removal is 1. It copies the added text verbatim, in reading order, without translating or guessing, and returns one region per added mark. The rules that took longest to get right:

  • One region is one mark. A logo and its words together; emoji at a line's ends belong to that line; lines on one carrier are one region; lines with photograph between them are separate boxes.
  • The box covers the carrier (chip, band, bubble, scrim, glow, shadow) out to its edge, not just the letters.
  • Protect what must stay. Plates, serial numbers, screens, meters and a device's own display come back as regions with physically_in_scene: true.
  • Self-check. Every copied line that lies on the photo must sit inside some box.

Step by step

Every stage is there because a photo needed it

The eraser holds FLUX.1-Fill with an object-removal LoRA, SAM 2.1, CRAFT and LaMa on one GPU. Each stage below was added after a specific failure on real listings, and each comes with the photo that showed the problem: the pipeline without the stage, then with it.

mask (what gets repainted)worker box (added text)protected (real-world writing)layer-2 repair

01

Gate: decide whether anything should be erased

Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite · ~1,500 tokens in, 12 out · one call on every photo

Why it's there: most text should not be erased. An advert built around its words has nothing underneath to restore, and a shop sign or a printed label belongs to the photo. Only text added on top of a real photograph leaves a hole worth filling. The three photos below get three different answers; each is the gate's own reply from the test set. On the 219-photo test set, gate plus worker reach 92.2% accuracy with a 7.5% false-positive rate.

02

Worker boxes, split into lines

worker · box_2d = [ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax] on a 0–1000 grid · one box per region and per line

Why it's there: when the worker draws one box around three lines, SAM2 is asked to outline "the object in this box" and returns the gate behind the text. The mask grows to the whole gate, and FLUX replaces the gate with a wall. Splitting bare text into its line boxes keeps SAM2 on the letters.

03

Refine and align the boxes

CRAFT word map · snap edges, widen emoji lines, re-align shifted replies

Why it's there: Gemini's coordinates drift. On 3–8% of regions a box is about one line off, and sometimes a whole reply is shifted half a line down. Edges that cut through a word leave its top behind. The refine step snaps each box onto the words CRAFT detects, using the box as the search area.

04

Starting mask: SAM2 ∪ change region ∪ CRAFT strokes

SAM 2.1 large, box prompts · alpha-matte change region · CRAFT character scores

Why it's there: SAM2 finds the shape of a chip or band, and the change region catches translucent panels, but thin white strokes on a busy brick wall slip through both. What they miss shows up as a faint line of letters in the result. Adding CRAFT's character strokes inside the boxes closes the gap.

05

Protect real-world writing, close the gaps

worker flag physically_in_scene · protected areas cut out of the stencil · rows between lines closed

Why it's there: the product's own writing is part of the goods. The worker marks a scale's display, a sign, a label or a licence plate as real-world, and those areas are cut out of the stencil and restored after every repaint. Gaps between caption lines are closed, so no thin strip of old background survives between two repainted lines.

06

Repaint with FLUX.1-Fill, blended outward only

FLUX.1-Fill-dev · objectRemoval LoRA r32 at 1.0 · guidance 30 · 30 steps · NF4 · crop long edge 768–1280 px

Why it's there: FLUX.1-Fill is the only FLUX variant with a real inpainting input (384 input channels: noise, masked image and mask). The removal LoRA is the restraint dial, and a positive prompt ("plain empty surface, seamless continuation…") gives guidance something to pull toward, because with an empty prompt the model's prior for a banner-shaped hole is more text.

The blend matters as much as the model. A Gaussian-feathered mask also softens inward, so thin strokes never reach full strength and a faint copy of the text survives in its original colour: 6–8% of the original showed through inside the mask. Taking the brighter of the mask and its blur keeps the inside at full strength and puts all of the soft edge outside.

07

Patch fix for flat surfaces

flags grain + a seam on smooth surroundings · 2 repaints through a disguised hole · kept only if the score drops ≥ 10%

Why it's there: on smooth sky or a plain wall, the first repaint can come back as a grainy rectangle in the exact shape of the watermark chip. The check finds it, repaints just that patch through a hole with a randomised edge (so FLUX doesn't see the chip's shape), and pastes the result strictly inside the original mask.

08

Layer 2: remove text the repaint invented

CRAFT inside the hole → SAM2 outlines what was invented → repaint inside the stencil · ran on 22 of 111 photos

Why it's there: FLUX fills a hole with what it expects to see there. Over an oven's dial it expects dial numbers, and it writes new ones into the hole where the caption was. CRAFT finds text inside the repainted hole, SAM2 extends it to the whole invented object, and that area is repainted again, strictly inside the original stencil.

09

Size gate: ship the original when the hole is too big

hold if the largest hole covers > 16% of the photo and > 25% of its centre · held 4 of 111

Why it's there: mask size was the strongest predictor of failure from the first human review on (95% of photos passed at 2–5% of the frame, none above 20%). When most of a product would have to be invented, the honest answer is the untouched photo. Here six lines of text cover most of a water-filter system. The repaint looks plausible at a glance, but every housing in it was invented, so the eraser ships the original.

When the first repaint is enough, and when it isn't

On most photos the checks after the first repaint all pass and nothing else runs. On about one in five, a later stage has to step in. Both animations are real runs, frame by frame.

Fitting the model

NF4: FLUX in a quarter of the memory

The FLUX transformer is 12 billion parameters, 23 GB in bf16. The eraser keeps it in NF4, the 4-bit NormalFloat format from QLoRA. Weights are stored as 4-bit codes on levels spaced for normally distributed values, in blocks of 64 with one scale per block, and those scales are quantized again (double quantization). Matrix multiplies still run in bf16: each block is dequantized on the fly.

# pipeline/erase.py: the reviewed loader
transformer = FluxTransformer2DModel.from_pretrained(
    FLUX_PATH, subfolder="transformer",
    torch_dtype=torch.bfloat16,
    quantization_config=BitsAndBytesConfig(
        load_in_4bit=True,
        bnb_4bit_quant_type="nf4",
        bnb_4bit_compute_dtype=torch.bfloat16,
        bnb_4bit_use_double_quant=True))
pipe = FluxFillPipeline.from_pretrained(
    FLUX_PATH, transformer=transformer, ...)
pipe.load_lora_weights(LORA, adapter_name="removal")

For deployment the work is done once, ahead of time. prepare_models.py saves the transformer already quantized (6.7 GB on disk) and the repaint prompt already encoded. The prompt never changes, so the 9.5 GB T5 text encoder is never loaded on the GPU node at all. Outputs are bit-identical to the reviewed run.

What NF4 changes in the picture

Same photo, same mask, same seed; only the precision differs. Across the comparison photos the median per-pixel difference is 5.28 levels out of 255, and side-by-side reviews could not tell which was which. The difference map, amplified 8×, is black everywhere except inside the repainted price chip, where the two differ only in the fine texture of the marble.

What it buys in use

  • Cheaper cards. The whole eraser fits on a 24 GB RTX A5000 or 4090; bf16 needs about 30 GB and a 48 GB card.
  • Room on an A6000. At 13.4 GiB one process leaves most of a 48 GB card free. A second copy adds at most about 10% throughput, because one copy already keeps the GPU about 95% busy.
  • Faster start. Reading 6.7 GB of prepared weights instead of 23 GB plus a 9.5 GB encoder, with no quantization step at start-up.
  • The catch: bitsandbytes 4-bit kernels need compute capability 7.5 or newer, so V100s are out.

That sample points to the next work. Three quarters of the bad results were designed graphics, such as text cards and adverts, that the gate should have left alone (step 01). The rest were an emoji over a licence plate, erased into an invented plate, and a caption across a vehicle that redrew part of it. Two gate rules would cover most of it: a designed graphic is never erased, and a sticker over a plate or a face is never erased.

On the eraser side, layer 2 sometimes leaves a faint outline where an invented object was, and two late stages (after FLUX in layer 2, and the plate carve) still paint with LaMa. Both are the next stages to replace.

Next

Real data, then a LoRA of its own

The repaint model is general. FLUX.1-Fill with an object-removal LoRA was trained to remove objects, not seller overlays from listing photos, and most of the later stages exist to catch what that mismatch produces: invented text in band-shaped holes, flat patches on smooth surfaces, outlines where a chip was.

1 · Gather real data in production

Keep every photo the system touches: the original, the worker's boxes, each stage's output, which checks fired and the reviewer's verdict. Keep the misses and the held photos too, and the many listing photos that carry no overlay at all.

2 · Train a LoRA for this job

Train a LoRA for FLUX.1-Fill on real listing photos. Real overlays pasted onto clean listing photos give before-and-after pairs with an exact answer, and reviewer-accepted outputs add the hard real cases.

3 · Shrink the repair stages

With a repaint that stops inventing text, layer 2, the patch fix and the two LaMa passes can shrink or go, which also cuts GPU time. The gate gets its two new rules from the sample above.
